## A Queue Made of Two Stacks

A stack hands back the most recent element; a queue hands back the oldest.
They are opposites, which makes it surprising that two stacks are enough to
build a queue.

The idea worth finding is that **reversing twice restores the original order**.

Complete the three methods of `StackQueue`:

| Method | What it must do |
|---|---|
| `void enqueue(int x)` | Add `x` to the queue. |
| `int dequeue()` | Remove and return the element that was added earliest. |
| `boolean isEmpty()` | Report whether the queue holds nothing. |

Two stacks are provided as fields, `inbox` and `outbox`, and a complete
`ArrayStack<T>` is provided above. **Use only those two stacks.** No arrays,
no lists, no second queue.

`dequeue` on an empty queue must throw a `RuntimeException`; the driver
catches it and prints `empty`.

### The cost is the hard part

`dequeue` must be **$O(1)$ amortized**. A single call may move many elements,
but the total work over a whole run of commands has to stay proportional to
the number of commands. A solution that touches the whole queue on every
`dequeue` gives the right answers and will not finish.
